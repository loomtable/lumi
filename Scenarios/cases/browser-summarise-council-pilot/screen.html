Riverton approves four-day week pilot for 1,200 council workers

Staff will work 32 hours for the same pay from January, but bin collections and libraries must keep their current hours.

By Priya Okafor · 6 min read

Riverton City Council voted 9–4 on Monday night to trial a four-day working week for about 1,200 office and depot staff, making it the largest such pilot by a city government in the region. The six-month trial starts on January 5 and will cut the standard week from 37 to 32 hours with no change in pay.

Council leader Marcus Ellery said the city spends roughly $2.1 million a year on agency staff to cover vacancies, and hoped a shorter week would help it hire and keep planners, engineers and social workers. "We are losing people to the private sector every month," he said.

Opposition councillors argued the pilot could reduce services. Under the motion, waste collection, libraries and the customer contact centre must keep their current opening hours, using staggered rotas. An independent panel from Northgate University will publish results in August 2027, measuring sick days, vacancies and resident complaints.

The trial will end early if missed bin collections rise more than 10 percent above last year's level for two months in a row.
