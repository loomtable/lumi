Eye colour

From OpenWiki, the free encyclopedia

Eye colour is a trait determined mainly by the amount and type of melanin in the front layers of the iris. Brown eyes have a lot of melanin; blue eyes have very little, and their colour comes from the way light scatters in the stroma.

Genetics

Eye colour is a polygenic trait, influenced by at least 16 genes. The two with the biggest effect are OCA2 and HERC2, which sit next to each other on chromosome 15. A single allele of HERC2 can reduce how much OCA2 is switched on, which lowers melanin and makes blue eyes more likely.

Because each parent passes on one copy of every gene, two brown-eyed parents can have a blue-eyed child if both carry the lighter variant. The old school rule that blue is simply "recessive" is a useful simplification but does not explain green or hazel eyes.
